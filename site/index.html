Solas sa seomra · grianstad an gheimhridhLight in the chamber: Newgrange at the winter solstice

Pick a morning and drag the time slider. The Sun’s position is computed for Newgrange. The gold window is the zone of sky from which direct sunlight was actually photographed in the chamber in 2020–21: the 53 positions measured in the NMS 2024 research report, shown as dots. When the Sun is inside that window, the beam lights the chamber.

Schematic, not to scale in height. The passage is about 19 m long and the chamber floor sits about 2 m above the entrance, so the light comes in through the roof-box above the door, not through the doorway (O’Kelly 1982; NMS 2024). O’Kelly saw the beam on 21 Dec 1969 from 8.58 to 9.15 GMT, 17 minutes in all. The “Neolithic tilt” button uses the Earth’s larger tilt c. 3200 BC, when the solstice Sun rose further south; Ray (1989) showed that the beam then reached the back of the chamber.

    TástálachaPre-registered tests

    Following the PA Marine approach, each test is written down, with its data, null model and pass rule, before anything is scored. No results have been scored yet. The full protocol is in RESEARCH.md and prereg/tests_v1.json.

    1. Wedge-tomb orientation (south-west / setting Sun?)

      The 309 wedge tombs whose SMR descriptions give a compass orientation, against a von Mises null and a random-rotation Monte Carlo (10,000 draws), testing whether they cluster within ±11.25° of the winter-solstice sunset azimuth at each site.

    2. Passage tombs facing solstice declinations

      The 43 SMR passage-tomb descriptions with compass orientations, plus any surveyed axes made available (Prendergast’s 136 are not published as a table), are converted to declinations using DSM horizons and tested for peaks at ±ε, 0° and ±(ε ± i) against random azimuths. The NMS 2024 result (global p = 0.896, no clustering) is the benchmark.

    3. Stone rows and the lunar standstills

      The 136 stone rows with recorded orientations, tested for clustering at the major and minor standstill declinations with the hill-target confound modelled. This is a replication of Ruggles (1994/96) on independent SMR data.

    4. Intervisibility vs chance

      Do passage tombs point at higher tombs more often than random siting predicts? This uses DSM viewsheds and a null that places the same number of tombs randomly on comparable terrain.

    5. Horizon targets

      Are stone-pair and stone-row axes aimed at local horizon maxima (peaks) more often than random azimuths are, given each site’s DSM profile?

      FúinnAbout

      PA Réalt is a project by Garry Lohan (mechanical engineer and lecturer, ATU Galway), about Irish monuments, astronomy and the Irish language. It is a sister site of PA Marine.

      This is an independent research prototype, not an official National Monuments Service, OPW or Heritage Council product. Many sites are on private land: check access and respect the monuments. Rays use a flat horizon except at the featured sites, which use a 30 m surface model, so they are indicative only.
