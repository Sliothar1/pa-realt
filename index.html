    Watch the light enter Newgrange →Solas sa seomra

    By Garry Lohan · Ag faire ar an spéir (Watching the sky)

    Solas sa seomra · grianstad an gheimhridhLight in the chamber: Newgrange at the winter solstice

    Pick a morning and drag the time slider. The Sun’s position is computed for Newgrange. The gold window is the zone of sky from which direct sunlight was actually photographed in the chamber in 2020–21: the 53 positions measured in the NMS 2024 research report, shown as dots. When the Sun is inside that window, the beam lights the chamber.

    Schematic, not to scale in height. The passage is about 19 m long and the chamber floor sits about 2 m above the entrance, so the light comes in through the roof-box above the door, not through the doorway (O’Kelly 1982; NMS 2024). O’Kelly saw the beam on 21 Dec 1969 from 8.58 to 9.15 GMT, 17 minutes in all. The “Neolithic tilt” button uses the Earth’s larger tilt c. 3200 BC, when the solstice Sun rose further south; Ray (1989) showed that the beam then reached the back of the chamber.

    Spéir na hoíche · an spéir inniuThe sky over Brú na Bóinne

    This is the real sky above Newgrange: 2,309 catalogue stars down to magnitude 5.3, with the Sun, Moon and planets computed for the chosen moment. The rim of the dome is the true local horizon, and the gold ticks show where the Sun rises and sets at the solstices and equinoxes.

      Solas na seachtaineLight this week

      Working out the light…

      Featured sites with a claimed or observed alignment in the next 14 days, with live sunrise or sunset times (Irish time). “Real horizon” is the moment the Sun’s upper edge clears the Copernicus 30 m skyline, which includes trees. “Flat” is the textbook time. The Newgrange window is the one photographed by the NMS. The other windows are our own (solstice ±7 days, equinox ±3 days).

      Éileamh agus fianaiseClaims vs evidence

      Some alignments are measured, repeatable and probably intended. Others are beautiful coincidences, or claims that fell apart when surveyed. Each card gives the claim, what was measured and our reading of it, with links to the sources.

      • Strong measured, repeatable, widely accepted
      • Observed the light effect is real; intent unproven
      • Debated experts disagree
      • Proposed single-site claim, untested
      • Not supported the survey or the statistics do not support it

      TástálachaPre-registered tests

      Following the PA Marine approach, each test is written down, with its data, null model and pass rule, before anything is scored. The tests were scored on 9 Oct 2026 with code frozen beforehand (commit c244e8f). Three found an effect and two found nothing, as summarised below. Full results · Torthaí → The full protocol is in RESEARCH.md and prereg/tests_v1.json.

      1. Wedge tombs and the setting Sun

        Wedge tombs whose SMR description says which way the tomb faces (“faces”, “opens to”, “entrance at”). 51 of the 309 records with a compass phrase qualify. Do more of them fall in the bin that holds the midwinter sunset than random directions would? Tested with 10,000 random-rotation draws, a V-test against 236°, and Cork and Kerry left out as a check.

      2. Passage tombs at solstice, equinox and standstill declinations

        34 passage tombs with a parsed facing direction, plus the 2 surveyed axes with published declinations (Newgrange and Cairn T). Each is converted to a declination through its own real horizon and scored against the 7 solar and lunar targets, with random directions as the comparison. The NMS 2024 national survey (136 tombs, global p = 0.896) is the benchmark.

      3. Stone rows and the lunar standstills

        119 stone rows with a parsed axis. Is the lower-horizon end near the Moon’s major or minor standstill declinations, compared with random directions and with directions aimed at horizon peaks? A replication of Ruggles (1994/96) on independent SMR data.

      4. Passage-tomb intervisibility

        All 216 mapped passage tombs, Republic and NI. Can each see another passage tomb within 10 km (line of sight over the real terrain)? The comparison is random points on similar terrain near each cemetery (1,000 draws).

      5. Horizon peaks

        245 stone-pair and stone-row axes. Do they point at a horizon peak (prominence of 0.5° or more) more often than random directions on the same site’s horizon?

        FúinnAbout

        PA Réalt is a project by Garry Lohan (mechanical engineer and lecturer · Google Scholar · LinkedIn), about Irish monuments, astronomy and the Irish language. It is a sister site of PA Marine.

        This is an independent research prototype, not an official National Monuments Service, OPW or Heritage Council product. Many sites are on private land: check access and respect the monuments. Rays and cards use each monument’s real horizon from the Copernicus 30 m surface model, which includes trees and buildings, so they are indicative only, not field surveys.
